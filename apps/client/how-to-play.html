How to Play Caravan (Fallout: New Vegas)

Caravan is a fast two-player tactical card game originally introduced in Fallout: New Vegas. Think of it as three simultaneous blackjack-style bidding wars where you aim for 21 to 26 while sabotaging your opponent with Jacks, Queens, Kings, and Jokers.

1. Objective & How to Win

Each player builds three caravans (columns of cards) directly across from their opponent's three caravans. Each caravan's bid is the sum of its number cards (Ace = 1, 2–10 = face value, plus any doubling from Kings).

  • A caravan is sold when its bid is between 21 and 26 (inclusive).
  • Any bid below 21 is under-sold, and any bid of 27 or higher is over-sold (busted) and does not count.
  • Each of the three competing pairs is won by whichever side has the higher sold bid.
  • The game ends as soon as all three competing pairs have a winner (no ties and no undecided pair). The player winning 2 or 3 tracks wins the match.
  • If a player runs out of cards in their hand and deck on their turn, their opponent wins immediately.
Important: You can still add cards to an already sold caravan (for instance, bumping 22 up to 26) to outbid your opponent, and you can win the game with only two active caravans if your opponent has sold the third.

2. Setup & Opening Round

Each player uses their own deck of at least 30 cards (standard play uses a 54-card deck with 2 Jokers; custom decks may mix card backs from multiple sets, with no duplicate card from the same set).

  1. Both players draw 8 cards to start.
  2. Over the first 3 alternating turns, each player must place one number card (Ace–10) face-up to start each of their three caravans.
  3. You do not draw replacement cards, discard cards, or play face cards during the opening round.
  4. If an opening hand has fewer than three number cards, it is automatically reshuffled and redrawn so you can always start all three caravans.
  5. Once all six caravans have one card, both players enter the main phase with 5 cards in hand.

3. What You Can Do on Your Turn

After the opening round, players alternate turns taking exactly one of three actions:

  • Play a card from your hand onto the table, then draw 1 card from your deck.
  • Discard a card from your hand, then draw 1 card from your deck.
  • Disband ("Discard Track") one of your non-empty caravans, clearing all its cards to the discard pile (no card drawn).

4. Number Cards, Suits & Direction

Number cards (Ace = 1, 2 through 10 = face value) can only be played on your own caravans.

  • The second number card in a caravan establishes its numerical direction: ascending or descending.
  • Every subsequent number card must either continue the direction (any suit) or match the suit of the caravan's last card.
  • Numbers do not need to be consecutive — jumping from 3 to 9 (ascending) or 10 to 4 (descending) is legal.
  • Playing a matching-suit card against the current direction is legal and reverses the caravan's direction going forward.
  • You may never place a number card directly on another card of the exact same value (for example, 10 on 10), even if the suits match.

5. Face Cards & Jokers

Unlike number cards, face cards and Jokers can be played on either your own or your opponent's caravans. They attach to the side of a number card and ignore suit/direction rules.

Card Where it plays Effect
Jack (J) Any number card on either side Removes that number card — along with any face cards or Jokers attached to it — to the discard pile.
Queen (Q) Last number card of any caravan only Reverses the caravan's ascending/descending direction and changes its active suit to the Queen's suit. Multiple Queens toggle back and forth.
King (K) Any number card on either side Doubles that number card's value. Multiple Kings stack multiplicatively (e.g. 10 + K = 20; 10 + K + K = 40). Great both for finishing your own 26 and for pushing an opponent over 26!
Joker Any number card on either side On an Ace: removes every other number card of that Ace's printed suit from both sides.
On a 2–10: removes every other card of that exact number from both sides.
The card the Joker sits on is spared. Queens changing a caravan's suit do not protect cards from an Ace-Joker.

6. Tricky Rules & Edge Cases

  • Three-attachment limit: A single number card can hold at most 3 face cards or Jokers. Once full, even a Jack cannot target it — only a Joker played on another card or disbanding the caravan can clear it.
  • Direction after removals (Rule 5.4): When a Jack or Joker removes a card from the middle of a track, direction recalculates from the remaining last two cards. If the removal leaves two identical numbers next to each other (for example, 6 → 7+Q → 6 losing the 7 to become 6 → 6), the caravan keeps its previous direction.
  • Ties do not end the game: If two tracks are won cleanly and the third is tied at 24–24, play continues until someone breaks the tie or disbands the tied track.

7. Quick Strategy Tips

  • Build around 10s, 8s, 6s, and Kings: Pairs like 10 + 10 + 6 = 26, 10 + K + 6 = 26, or 8 + K + 10 = 26 hit the unbeatable maximum bid of 26 in just three cards.
  • Use Kings offensively: Dropping a King on an opponent's 8, 9, or 10 pushes their 21–26 caravan over 26, forcing them to waste a Jack or disband the entire track.
  • Don't over-commit to all three tracks at once: You only need to win two tracks while the third is decided. Sometimes letting your opponent win one track cheaply lets you lock down the other two at 25–26 for an immediate win.
  • Use the custom Deck Builder: Against AI (or in private rooms with custom decks enabled), trimming low cards (2–5) and stocking 6s, 8s, 10s, Jacks, and Kings creates an ultra-consistent 30-card deck.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

How do you win a game of Caravan in Fallout: New Vegas?

Reach a bid between 21 and 26 on at least two of your three caravans with a higher score than the facing caravan, once every one of the three pairs has a non-tied winner. Running your opponent out of cards also wins immediately.

Why didn't the game end when I reached 26 on two caravans?

Check the third track: if neither player has sold it (both under 21 or over 26) or both players are tied at the same sold bid (such as 23 vs 23), the game cannot end yet. Either sell the third track, break the tie, or let your opponent sell it.

What does the Joker do on an Ace vs a number card?

On an Ace, the Joker wipes every other number card of that Ace's printed suit from both players' caravans. On a 2–10, it wipes every other card of that same number from both sides. The target card itself always survives.
